**What the law requires.** No Oregon statute requires the public to be told of a sewage release. The duty is a condition of every wastewater permit the Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) issues: when an overflow threatens public health, the permittee must "alert the public, health agencies and other affected entities" by the procedures in its permit or its own Emergency Response and Public Notification Plan (DEQ's general permit conditions). No deadline and no channel are set, so each operator decides how and where to tell the public. The operator reports the overflow by telephone to the Oregon Emergency Response System within 24 hours and to DEQ in writing within five days (the same conditions; Oregon Administrative Rules 340-142-0040 for spills). No period after a discharge is set during which the water is to be avoided. Three Oregon permits have combined sewers (sewage and rainwater in one pipe): Portland's, with 36 open overflow outfalls to the Willamette River and the Columbia Slough; Astoria's, with 38 to the Columbia River and Youngs Bay; and Corvallis's, with 4 (U.S. Environmental Protection Agency inventory).

**What is published.** The City of Portland's Environmental Services publishes an advisory as a news article, with an RSS feed, for each combined sewer overflow and for the sewage releases it reports: the start, the stretch of water ("the Willamette River downstream of the Ross Island Bridge" for an overflow), the advice to avoid contact during the overflow and for 48 hours after it stops, and the advisory's expiry. Between November 21, 2025 and October 22, 2026 it published 13 such advisories (4 for combined sewer overflows), each overflow advisory between 25 minutes and 14 hours after the overflow began (read November 7, 2026). The City also shows the current overflow advisory and the fill of its Big Pipe tunnels every 15 minutes, and sends the advisories by e-mail. DEQ published a map layer of every spill reported to the Oregon Emergency Response System and verified by DEQ from July 2013 to March 27, 2025: 2,371 sewage spills, each with DEQ's point, the date and time of the report and, for 1,317 of them, a volume in gallons. The Oregon Health Authority lists its advisories and precautions for toxic algae blooms with an issue date and a point (5 advisories and 34 precautions on November 7, 2026, among them a permanent advisory on the South Umpqua and Umpqua rivers), and from mid-May to mid-September posts the status of the coastal beaches it samples for bacteria.

**What is not published.** Nothing has been published of the reports operators made to the Oregon Emergency Response System or to DEQ since March 27, 2025: DEQ's layer stops there and no newer list was found. DEQ's layer gives the date and time of each report, not when the spill began or ended, and names a receiving water for 323 of the 2,371 spills. Astoria and Corvallis publish no notice of their overflows on the web, and the other operators' own pages were not searched one by one; Oak Lodge Water Services posts each of its overflows as a news item. Portland's advisories give no coordinates, and its releases from private property name no operator.

**What stands in the way.** With no statute and no channel set, a member of the public has no single place to learn of a sewage release in Oregon outside Portland. The regulator's reports are taken by telephone and on paper, and DEQ's only public record of them is a layer frozen in March 2025. Some operators' notices reach only e-mail subscribers or signs at the water.

**Known problems in the data.** DEQ's layer types the water by hand: placeholders ("NULL", "None", "Unknown") on most rows, misspellings ("Tualitan River", "Coquile River") and towns after the water ("Columbia River, Astoria"). Portland's RSS feed dates each item by its page's last update, which can be the advisory's expiry, so the advisory's own Published line is used instead. Portland's articles give times in words ("around noon on December 9"), sometimes only the day, and one advisory reports two releases nine days apart.

**What this site does about it.** Each of Portland's advisories is shown as a sewage report from the start its text gives (or the time crews responded, or the day only) to the end it gives; where the advisory advises avoiding contact for 48 hours after the overflow stops, those 48 hours are shown as the City's own window, then this site's caution to 72 hours. An overflow advisory that gives only its expiry is drawn to that expiry. A release the City says is still running is shown in effect for seven days, then as "end not confirmed". An overflow advisory is placed on the City's combined sewer outfalls on the water it names; a release that names no water is listed on this page and placed on none, and one the City says reached no water (sewage held in an excavation, or returned to the sewer) is listed but not placed. DEQ's 2013 to 2025 spills are kept as history: each is placed on the water it names in its own county, or at DEQ's point, and those whose record lists only soil, pavement or a building as affected are listed but not placed; because DEQ gives no end, each carries this site's caution for 14 days from its report and is then shown as "end not confirmed". The Health Authority's bloom advisories and precautions are shown as algal bloom advisories, in their own colour, for as long as it lists them, with its own word for the level. Times are read in Pacific time, and in Mountain time in Malheur County north of township 35 South. The callers' accounts, the incident titles and the street addresses in DEQ's records, and the house numbers and contact details in Portland's pages, are not reproduced. The rain-based caution this site computes from radar applies at Portland's, Astoria's and Corvallis's overflow outfalls.
