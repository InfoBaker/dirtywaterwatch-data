**What the law requires.** A Kansas regulation requires an emergency or accidental discharge of sewage that a valid permit does not provide for to be reported to the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE) immediately, and a planned bypass of a treatment plant to be approved by KDHE at least seven days ahead (K.A.R. 28-16-27). No Kansas statute or rule requiring the public to be told of a sewer overflow was found, and none sets a period after one during which the water is to be avoided. KDHE issues a stream advisory, asking people to avoid contact with a stretch of a stream, river or lake, when it judges one needed after a discharge, and rescinds it by a later release. Three permits hold combined sewers (sewage and rainwater in one pipe): the Unified Government of Wyandotte County and Kansas City, Kansas, Topeka and Atchison; they are expected to notify the public under their permits and, for the Unified Government, its consent decree with the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, whose notification clauses were not read for this page.

**What is published.** The City of Leavenworth posts a page for each of its sewer overflows: 28 pages from April 2020 to April 2026, each with the time staff were told of or found the overflow, often the time it stopped, the estimated gallons, the creek affected where named, and the City's advice to stay away from the creek until its tests and signs say otherwise. KDHE's news feed carries its stream advisories as they are issued and rescinded. KDHE's harmful algal bloom map lists the lakes under a bloom Hazard, Warning or Watch: on November 14, 2026, 15 lakes, 7 under a Warning and 8 under a Watch.

**What is not published.** The reports operators make to KDHE are not published, and no other Kansas city was found to post its overflows. KDHE's news feed holds only about the last month of releases, and on November 14, 2026 it held a single stream advisory release, a rescission of an advisory issued before that month; KDHE's release pages and its earlier news cannot be read automatically, and KDHE publishes no list or map layer of the stream advisories in effect, so an advisory issued before this site began reading the feed is not shown. The bloom map gives the date KDHE last updated it, not when each advisory began or ended. KDHE's public map of reported spills holds few sewage reports (40 since 1983, mostly septic haulers and spills onto soil) and is not a record of sewer overflows.

**What stands in the way.** KDHE's website refuses automated reading, so its release pages, its bloom page and its incident report form can be read only by hand; Topeka's news site, where it has posted stream advisories, does the same. The reports the regulation requires go to KDHE's district offices by telephone and are seen only through a records request.

**Known problems in the data.** Leavenworth's pages give the time staff were told of an overflow, not when it began, and some give a time without a.m. or p.m. or name only "the creek". KDHE's feed gives its times with the same offset in summer and winter. The bloom map lists one lake twice.

**What this site does about it.** A Leavenworth page is dated to the time it gives, the time staff were told of or found the overflow and so the latest it can have begun, not necessarily its start, and to the day where its time cannot be read; its stop time is used as the end, and its advice is quoted without inventing a date for it. A page that names its creek is placed on that creek within Leavenworth County; one that names none is listed with its county and placed on no water. A KDHE stream advisory is shown as KDHE's own advisory from its release, on the waters it names, until KDHE rescinds it; once the feed no longer holds the release, it is shown for 14 days after the last read that confirmed it and then marked as not reconfirmed, since a rescission can no longer be seen. KDHE gives no point: the advisory is marked on the water it names inside the county KDHE names where this site has that water mapped there, and otherwise it is listed with its county and its place on the map is not known. A rescission whose advisory was issued before this site read the feed is not shown, since its start is unknown. A bloom advisory is shown from the first time this site read it on KDHE's map until the first time it no longer appears, both marked as this site's reads, not KDHE's dates; if the map cannot be read, it is shown for 14 days after the last read that listed it. Sherman, Wallace, Greeley and Hamilton counties keep Mountain time and their reports are read in it. Telephone and house numbers on the City's pages are left out; each Leavenworth report links the City's yearly list of overflow reports, since the page for the overflow itself carries a street address, and each KDHE advisory links its release. Combined sewer outfalls are shown from EPA's inventory, with the rain caution at each.
