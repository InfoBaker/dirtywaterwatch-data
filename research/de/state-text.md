**What the law requires.** Anyone who causes an environmental release in Delaware, which for sewage means 10,000 gallons or more, must report it to the Department of Natural Resources and Environmental Control as soon as it is known; the Department must post it on its website no later than one business day after the report, and within 12 hours of being informed notify the district's legislators and the neighbours and community groups within five miles who have registered (7 Del. C. 6002(20), 6014(b) and 6028(a)). Discharges in compliance with a permit are exempt, so Wilmington's combined sewer overflows, the state's only ones, are not reported this way: each of its 41 open outfalls is marked by a sign. No period after a release is set during which the water is to be avoided. The Department's Recreational Water Program samples ocean, bay and inland beaches in summer and posts advisories and closures.

**What is published.** The Department's public release list (Environmental Release Notification System, read on November 14, 2026) holds every release reported to it, of every substance, about 7,480 since 2002, each with a detail page: the release and report times, the location, the facility, the substance, whether it went to ground, water or air, an estimated quantity and the Department's notes. 1,295 of them are sewage or sanitary-wastewater releases by the substance the list gives, from March 2002 to October 18, 2026: 25 to 43 a year since 2017 and 15 in 2026. The form marks 474 of them as reaching water and 815 as reaching ground only; 916 give a quantity in gallons, with a median of 250 gallons, and 73 of those are 10,000 gallons or more. The report reached the Department a median of 24 minutes after the release time it gives. The Department's advisory history on the state's open-data portal (data.delaware.gov, read on November 14, 2026) holds its beach advisories and closures since 2001 with its own start and end dates; 2026 brought no advisory, only five notices that a beach was being resampled after a high result. Its map shows the current status of 47 sampling stations.

**What is not published.** The release list gives no end time, no map point and no posting time, and names a water only in the Department's notes (103 of the 1,295). Most releases name no operator: the facility is often given as "spill location" or "roadway". Wilmington's combined sewer overflows are not listed at all, and no overflow notice, list or alert from the City was found. Releases under 10,000 gallons appear only when someone reports them, which the law does not require. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's overflow records held no Delaware report on November 14, 2026.

**What stands in the way.** The permit exemption leaves the state's only combined sewers without any published overflow record. The Department's release alerts by e-mail, fax or telephone need an account; the public list carries the same releases.

**Known problems in the data.** The list mixes every substance, and "wastewater" can be sewage or industrial: 78 wastewater releases from food plants, chemical plants and similar sources and 101 that nothing ties to a sewer system are left out, as are 8 of treated effluent. Locations are free text; the town or ZIP code gives the county for all but 153. Water names in the notes are sometimes misspelled ("Nammans Creek").

**What this site does about it.** A release is kept as a sewage report when its substance names sewage, septic waste or grey water, or untreated or partially treated wastewater, or wastewater whose facility, location or notes name a sewer system (a pump station, a manhole, a force main, a treatment plant, a county or town's public works). It is shown from the release time the Department gives, in Eastern time; because the list gives no end, it is shown with this site's caution for 14 days from the report, then with its end not confirmed until 30 days after it began; a release whose time is given as midnight is read as the day only and shown with caution for 72 hours from the start of that day. A release is placed on the water its notes name; one the form marks to ground only, with no water in its notes, is listed with its county and placed on no water; one whose notes name a water while the form says ground only is placed there with this site's caution only. No street is mapped. A release at a private home is listed with its county and town only, and names and telephone numbers in the notes are removed; the Department's list links each full report. A beach advisory or closure is shown as the Department's own between its start and end dates, at its station's point; a resampling notice is shown with this site's caution, never as an advisory. Wilmington's 41 combined sewer outfalls show this site's rain caution after heavy rain.
