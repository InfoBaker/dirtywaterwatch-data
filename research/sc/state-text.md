**What the law requires.** South Carolina law requires a wastewater utility to report a significant spill, 5,000 gallons or more of untreated or partially treated sewage, to the Department of Environmental Services by telephone within 24 hours and in writing within five days, and to give public notice of it within 24 hours of discovering it (S.C. Code 48-1-95(B) and (E)). The law names no channel for that notice; the Department gives it when the utility does not. Spills caused by natural disasters, a third party's direct act or other acts of God are excluded. The Department's guidance also asks utilities to report any overflow that reaches a water. No period after a spill is set during which the water is to be avoided. South Carolina has no combined sewers. The Department samples about 122 points on the coast's beaches from May 1 to October 1 and posts swimming advisories, and posts watches and advisories for harmful algal blooms.

**What is published.** The City of Florence posts a notice for each sanitary sewer overflow of 5,000 gallons or more on its [news page](https://www.cityofflorencesc.gov/city-news): five from March 2024 to October 2025 when the page was read on November 14, 2026 (this site's count), all on Jeffries Creek, each giving the day the City learned of or responded to the overflow, its volume or that it was more than 5,000 gallons, and the City's advice. The Department's beach map (the [S.C. Beach Access Guide](https://gis.des.sc.gov/beachaccess/)) shows the current advisory at each of its 123 monitoring stations: on November 14, 2026, by this site's count of the map, 21 long-term advisories, 18 on the Grand Strand in Horry County and 3 at Edisto Beach, which stand from season to season where stormwater makes high bacteria likely after rain; short-term advisories, which usually last a day or two, are posted in season. The Department's [bloom page](https://des.sc.gov/programs/bureau-water/aquatic-science/harmful-algal-blooms) and map listed one watch on November 14, 2026, at Cedar Point Cove on J. Strom Thurmond Reservoir, issued October 16, 2026.

**What is not published.** The Department publishes no list of the spill reports it receives; its training material shows an overflow search among its online permitting system's public services, but that application could not be read without a browser session. Other utilities give the required notice in their own ways, and their pages were not searched one by one. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's overflow records held no South Carolina report on November 14, 2026. Florence's notices give neither the start nor the end of an overflow, and the City publishes no notice when its advice ends. The beach map gives no dates, and a separate map of freshwater swimming sites gives no date or status and is not explained by the Department, so it is not shown here.

**What stands in the way.** The law leaves the channel to each utility, so there is no one place to look, and a storm's overflows can fall under the acts-of-God exclusion and need no notice. Charleston Water System posts overflow news on its own site, which did not answer this site's requests in November 2026; it has once rewritten an earlier article for a later break, so its articles cannot be read one per overflow.

**Known problems in the data.** Florence spells the creek both Jeffries and Jefferies. Its figure "more than 5,000 gallons" is a lower bound, and its 972,000 gallons of July 2024 is combined stormwater and wastewater. A Florence notice of October 2025 dates the City's response as "yesterday".

**What this site does about it.** A Florence notice gives no start for the overflow, so none is shown: the report is dated by the day the City says it received notice of or responded to the overflow, in Eastern time, and shown with this site's caution for 14 days from that day, then with its end not confirmed until 30 days after it. The City's advice to avoid the creek is shown separately, as the City's own, from the day of the notice that carries it, for 14 days after the City last published it, and then as not reconfirmed; when the City's notice no longer carries the advice, it is shown as ended between the two readings of the page. The City's figure is kept as given, a lower bound as a lower bound. A notice is placed on the creek it names; no street near a manhole is mapped. A page the City rewrites for a later overflow is kept as a second report, never in place of the first. A beach advisory is shown as the Department's own at its station's point while the map shows it, dated from this site's first reading of the map, since the map gives no date; a station marked "No Advisory" is shown as no report. A bloom watch or advisory is shown as the Department's own at the centre of the area the Department delineated, never as the whole lake or reservoir, since the Department names a cove or arm; where two delineations on one water share a date and nothing ties each to its cove, no point is given, and the watch is listed on the reservoir's page with its place on the map not known. South Carolina has no combined sewer outfalls, so this site's rain caution does not apply here.
