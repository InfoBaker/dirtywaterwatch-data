**What the law requires.** Every sanitary sewer overflow and treatment plant overflow under a Wisconsin permit, from a public or a private system, must be announced to the public promptly, by the means the operator's emergency response plan names (s. NR 210.21(5), Wis. Adm. Code). The Department of Natural Resources' instructions set the minimum: when the overflow reaches surface water, a daily newspaper must be told. No web posting and no deadline in hours are required. The operator reports the overflow to the Department within 24 hours and on the Department's form within five business days, with the location, duration, volume, receiving water and how the public was told. The two combined sewer operators (sewage and rainwater in one pipe), the Milwaukee Metropolitan Sewerage District and the City of Superior, are in the Great Lakes Basin and must give an electronic public notice within four hours of becoming aware of an overflow (40 CFR 122.38). Plants on tribal land hold permits from the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency instead, and report to it under federal rules. No Wisconsin rule sets a period after a discharge during which the water should be avoided.

**What is published.** There is no statewide list of sewer overflows. The Milwaukee Metropolitan Sewerage District posts a Storm Update on its news page with what it reported to the Department after an overflow of its regional system: the dates, often the volume, sometimes the start and end times and the water, and a link to its letter. It also posts a notice when sewage is spilled at one of its treatment plants or released from its sewers. Its news page held 21 such posts on November 16, 2026 (18 storm updates and overflow letters and 3 spill or release notices), from June 2018 to September 2026, giving 29 reports: 13 combined sewer overflows, 14 separate sewer overflows or overflows not described as combined, and 2 spills at treatment plants. The most recent was an overflow of 331,500 gallons into Lincoln Creek on September 3, 2026, posted on September 8. Eleven of the posts give the overflow's own date or start, and those appeared one to six days after it began, four days at the median; the other ten give only the storm's dates (Milwaukee Metropolitan Sewerage District, news page, read November 16, 2026). The district also lists the waters a combined sewer overflow may affect: the Milwaukee and Menomonee rivers, Lake Michigan's harbor and nearshore, and their boat launches and parks (Milwaukee Metropolitan Sewerage District, combined sewer overflow impacted-waters page, read November 3, 2026). The City of Superior's combined sewer page has a table for each overflow's location, water, start, end and volume, and its annual notice reports no combined sewer overflow in 2025 (City of Superior, combined sewer overflow page and 2025 annual notice, revised July 2026, as found on November 9, 2026); on November 16 the table had no filled row. The Department of Natural Resources lists beach advisories and closures, with the reason (E. coli or algae) and the date of the advisory, in its Wisconsin Beach Health system, for the Great Lakes beaches and the inland beaches local health departments report. On November 16, 2026, it listed 441 beaches on its map: one algae advisory, at Gile Flowage Beach from September 17, one algae closure, at Lake Kegonsa State Park Beach from September 11, and the rest closed for the season (Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources, Wisconsin Beach Health, read November 16, 2026). The Department also lists open spill incidents of every substance, sewage among them, with the date, county, municipality, substance and cause.

**What is not published.** The Department publishes none of the overflow reports it receives, from Milwaukee or anywhere else, and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's national overflow records held no Wisconsin report in their files of November 13, 2026, current or historical. Outside Milwaukee, an overflow is announced in a newspaper or, by some cities and villages, as a single news item or press release on their own sites, with no list to follow. The Milwaukee district's posts come with its letter to the Department, not within four hours, give no outfall and seldom a water; its letters are often scanned images. The district's year-to-date overflow counter is not a record of when an overflow happened, and the district says its figures can be corrected. Overflows in the member municipalities' own sewers are mentioned in the district's posts only by name.

**What stands in the way.** The overflow reports sit in the Department's Switchboard system, which only permittees can open. The City of Milwaukee Health Department's beach report answers automated readers with a browser challenge (HTTP 403, November 15 and 16, 2026), and we do not read it; its beaches appear in the Department's beach system when the Department lists them. The Department's spill list mixes every substance and does not say which incidents are sewer overflows. The Milwaukee district's 2018 public notification plan gives its notice procedures only as scanned pages. No statewide algal-bloom advisory list was found; the Department of Health Services publishes health information, and blooms at beaches appear in the beach reports.

**Known problems in the data.** The Milwaukee district's posts are written differently from storm to storm: some give a combined volume for combined and separate sewer overflows together, some only the storm's dates, and some say "sewer overflows" without saying which kind. The dates a post gives are the storm's, so the last of them is not taken as the end of the overflow.

**What this site does about it.** Each Storm Update is kept as a dated report from the district, in Central time, with the dates, times, volume and waters it states and nothing more. A report with a stated end is shown to its end, then with our caution (72 hours; 96 on lakes, ponds and reservoirs), since the law sets no period; a report dated to the day only, with no end, shows caution for 72 hours from the start of that day, then is marked "End not confirmed" until 30 days after it began; one with a time but no end shows caution for 14 days from its report, then the same. A post that gives only the storm's dates, not the overflow's, is not given a start: it shows caution for 14 days from its publication, then is marked "End not confirmed" until 30 days after it. Counted at the moment each post appeared, 22 of the 29 reports were still showing caution: 18 give no start for the overflow and one gives a time but no end, so their caution runs from the post itself, and three were posted within their caution. For the other 7 of them (six dated to the day and one with a stated end) the caution had passed before the post appeared; every report stays in the water's history. A combined sewer overflow that names no outfall is shown on every water the district's outfalls discharge to; one that names a water is shown on that water at the district's outfalls there. A separate sewer overflow that names no water is listed with the district and placed on no water, and so is a spill the district says reached no water. A water named in a post is matched only to a water in Milwaukee County. A post counts both kinds of overflow apart where it gives their volumes apart; a volume given for both together is kept as written. The combined sewer outfalls in Milwaukee and Superior show our rain caution after heavy rain. We show each beach advisory and closure the Department lists, at the beach, from the Department's date of the advisory, for as long as the Department lists it; an advisory or closure for algae is shown as an algal bloom, apart from sewage, and a lake is never coloured whole for one beach. A lack of recent sample data does not cancel an advisory or closure the Department lists: we keep showing it. A beach the Department lists as open or closed for the season has no report here; that is not a finding about its water. A reason that names both bacteria and algae is shown once, at the beach, as both. An advisory at one of the beaches the Department's report lists without a map point is shown in Wisconsin's list with its beach and nearest town; its place on the map is not known, and we do not invent one. Not collected yet: the Department's spill list, the City of Superior's overflow table (no filled row has appeared to test a reader on), the Milwaukee district's overflows page and its post-storm reports, the City of Milwaukee Health Department's beach report, Public Health Madison and Dane County's beach page, and the four-hour notices the two combined sewer operators may give by text, e-mail or social media.
