**What the law requires.** Indiana's combined sewer rule (327 IAC 5-2.1) requires every community whose sewers carry sewage and rainwater in one pipe to notify the affected public, local health departments, drinking water suppliers with intakes within ten river miles downstream, and anyone who asks after its annual March newspaper notice, whenever an overflow is occurring or is expected from rain, in whatever manner it agrees with each recipient, and to post signs at boat ramps, bridges, parks and other access points warning that sewage may be in the water during and for several days after rain or snowmelt. The rule names no web page and no deadline. In the Lake Michigan and Lake Erie basins, a second rule (327 IAC 5-2.2) adds the federal one (40 CFR 122.38): a public notice within four hours of becoming aware of an overflow, a follow-up within seven days and a yearly notice, by the means each operator's plan names. Each combined sewer outfall's overflows are totalled every month on a discharge monitoring report. Sanitary sewer overflows, treatment plant bypasses and sewage basement backups are reported to the Indiana Department of Environmental Management (IDEM) within 24 hours; no rule requires the public to be told of them. The law sets no period after a discharge during which the water is to be avoided. IDEM's map lists 981 combined sewer outfall points on 110 permits, of which 578 are active, 85 active with discharges prohibited, and 318 inactive (November 14, 2026).

**What is published.** The operators' monthly totals for each combined sewer outfall (the number of discharges, the hours and the million gallons) are published by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency from their discharge monitoring reports: 4,484 outfall-months with a discharge from 73 of the 92 permits with an open combined sewer outfall, January 2025 to October 2026 (read November 14, 2026), each appearing a median of 24 days after the month ends. Indianapolis's reports run to December 2025; its 2026 months had not appeared. IDEM's own reports of sewer overflows and bypasses do not reach the federal overflow records yet; the federal extract of November 12, 2026 holds one Indiana report, a bypass at Shelbyville's treatment plant on August 12 and 13, 2026 (5,055,000 gallons, Big Blue River). Some operators publish more: Fort Wayne posts a summary of the past seven days' overflows each Wednesday; Waterloo posts each overflow's start and end notices; Mishawaka posts its alerts on an X account and a yearly table of overflows by date and outfall; South Bend and Hammond post yearly notices and monthly reports; La Porte posts a current status and a list of past overflows with volumes. IDEM's algae program publishes each beach sample it takes at state park and reservoir beaches, with IDEM's advisory level for the sample (1,717 samples at 29 beaches, 2010 to September 14, 2026).

**What is not published.** IDEM publishes no list of the overflow and bypass reports it receives; each is filed with its facility's documents in IDEM's Virtual File Cabinet, a document search with no list or feed, and an archive of 2005 to 2016 reports is posted as county lists. The notices the combined sewer rule requires go by e-mail, text or telephone to those who asked: South Bend, Hammond and Indianapolis's Citizens Energy Group send them to subscribers, and none keeps a public list of them. Most operators' own pages were not searched one by one. The Lake Michigan beach advisories in IDEM's BeachAlert portal can be read only after signing in. The Department of Natural Resources' park alerts carry a standing general note on blue-green algae at most state park lakes ("Found in lakes year round"), which is general advice, not a report of a bloom.

**What stands in the way.** The law's own channel is a private message to each person who asks, so no public record of an overflow is required outside the Great Lakes basins. Fort Wayne's, Kendallville's and Speedway's pages refuse automated reading, La Porte's server did not answer this site on November 14, 2026, Mishawaka's alerts are on a site that asks readers to sign in, and BeachAlert's records require a sign-in. Waterloo's notices are PDF files this site cannot yet read. The monthly totals arrive weeks after the month and give no day.

**Known problems in the data.** The monthly totals are each operator's own figures: some are measured and some modelled, and the reports do not say which. One outfall-month was filed as 28,482 million gallons in 15 hours (September 2026), almost certainly gallons entered as million gallons; it is kept as filed in the text and not counted as a volume. 136 of IDEM's outfall points have no location (131 of them inactive). The federal outfall inventory lists 14 open outfalls that IDEM's map does not, and IDEM lists a few that the federal inventory does not. IDEM's beach samples give the date of the sample, not the dates an advisory was posted or lifted.

**What this site does about it.** Each outfall's monthly totals are shown with the monthly mark on the outfall's water for the month they cover, never as a dated discharge or in the colours of one, and are never added to the discharge counts; a month an operator reports with no discharge clears the mark. Every open combined sewer outfall on IDEM's map (669 outfalls on 92 permits, with the federal inventory's where IDEM's map has none) carries this site's rainfall caution: when radar rainfall at an outfall passes the threshold, the water below it is marked with a caution, since the operators' own notices are not published. The federal overflow report is shown from its start to its end, then with this site's caution (72 hours; 96 on lakes, ponds and reservoirs), since the law sets no period. IDEM's beach samples are shown apart from sewage, in IDEM's words: a sample at an advisory level appears in the history for its sample date, and only the latest sample at a beach, when it is recent, is shown as a current advisory, from the day this site first read it until a later sample replaces it; a Low Risk sample is not shown as anything. The operators' pages this site can read (South Bend, Hammond, Mishawaka, La Porte, Waterloo, Goshen, Evansville) are checked daily for changes. Outfalls IDEM marks inactive carry no caution; those marked active with discharges prohibited keep it, since an unpermitted overflow from them is still possible. Times are shown in Eastern time, and in Central time in the twelve counties of the northwest and southwest. House numbers in IDEM's outfall descriptions are not reproduced here.
