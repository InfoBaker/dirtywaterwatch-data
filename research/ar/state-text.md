**What the law requires.** Every discharge permit in Arkansas requires the permittee to report to the Division of Environmental Quality, within 24 hours of becoming aware of it and in most cases in writing within five days, any noncompliance that may endanger health or the environment, including "Any Sanitary Sewer Overflow (SSO), no matter how small", any unanticipated bypass of a treatment plant, any upset and any unpermitted discharge "regardless of whether it reaches waters of the state". Permittees file these reports on the Division's online forms. No Arkansas statute or rule requires a permittee or the Division to tell the public of an overflow, and the law sets no period after an overflow during which the water is to be avoided. Arkansas has no combined sewers. Little Rock Water Reclamation Authority's sewer overflow response plan, which grew out of a settlement, provides for warning signs and public notification at its overflows. The Division issues recreational use health advisories for harmful algal blooms, and the Department of Health's Swim Beach Program advises beach managers on opening and closing swim beaches.

**What is published.** The Division publishes the filed reports in its Reported Noncompliance Incident Data search, which the page states is current to the previous day. From January 1, 2025 to November 14, 2026 it lists 2,398 reports from sewer systems and sewage plants (1,575 in 2025, 823 in 2026, 44 of them in October 2026): 2,295 sanitary sewer overflows, 48 unpermitted discharges, 35 bypasses, 9 upsets and 11 with no type. Each gives the permit and facility, a location described in words, the days the overflow started and stopped, the day the Division received the report (a median of one day after the overflow began), the estimated volume in gallons, the cause, the action taken and what the filer says it reached: a stream (670 reports), public land only (721), private property (771), a basement (23) or the treatment plant (111). Little Rock Water Reclamation Authority filed 283 of them, the City of Fort Smith 247 and the City of Hot Springs 217. The filer ticked "Public Notification" among the actions taken on 550. The Division's harmful algal bloom file lists 52 advisories since 2014 with the days each was posted and lifted, the county and the nearest town; four were in effect on November 14, 2026: Lake Hamilton (posted July 24, 2026), Lake June (July 31), Bois d'Arc Lake (August 12) and Mom's Lake (September 22). The Department of Health's Swim Beach Program page lists the swim beaches it has closed by name: one, Jefferson Ridge West, on November 14, 2026.

**What is not published.** The incident search gives no time of day, no coordinates, no county and no field for the water an overflow reached: a water is known only when the filer names one in the location text, as 238 reports do. Ninety reports give no day the overflow stopped. The search does not show the notice an operator gave when it ticked "Public Notification". The Division also offers a Microsoft Access copy of the reports, last updated August 13, 2025. The bloom file gives no advisory level, and a point for only one advisory, written into its name. The Department of Health's list gives no date, reason or water for a closed beach. The Corps of Engineers' Little Rock District page of swim beach closures refused this site's automated requests on November 14, 2026. The operators' own pages were not searched one by one.

**What stands in the way.** No law requires anyone to tell the public of an overflow, so the Division's search, without the water affected, is the public record of nearly every overflow in the state. The search is a web form: its Text, Excel and PDF exports are generated for each query and are not used here, and it answers for one type of report at a time or for all types together, so this site reads it by date window and checks each window's count against the count the page states. The page shows no licence or terms of use.

**Known problems in the data.** The Division lists some reports twice under the same confirmation number (26 repeated rows from January 2025 to November 2026); each copy is kept but counted once. Nine reports give a stop day before the start or a mistyped year, nine reached the Division more than 120 days after the overflow began, and a few volumes are written as a rate or a range. The search also lists reports from industrial permits; those that are not sewer overflows (26 from paper and packaging mills, a gravel mine, a brick plant and other plants) are left out here. Starred rows of the bloom archive are a partner agency's advisory or a confirmed bloom for which the Division issued no advisory, and 18 older advisories give "N/A" for the day lifted.

**What this site does about it.** A report is shown from the day the overflow started to the day it stopped, then with this site's caution (72 hours; 96 on lakes, ponds and reservoirs), since the law sets no period; a report with no stop day is shown with this site's caution for 72 hours from the start of the day it began, then with its end not confirmed until 30 days after it began. Bypasses, upsets and unpermitted discharges are shown as sewage reports like the overflows. A report whose filer says it stayed on public land, on private property or in a basement is kept and listed but never placed on a water. A report that names a water in its location, and whose filer does not say it stayed on land, is placed on that water, in the county of its permit's treatment plant; every other report is listed with its operator and placed on no water: this site does not place it at the operator's plant and does not guess a location from the street. Dates are read in Central time. The Division's bloom advisories are shown as its own, from the day posted to the day lifted. An advisory for a whole lake colours the lake it names, with a marker at the lake's point in the U.S. Geological Survey's place names where one exists; an advisory for one bay or arm of a lake, or for a stream the Division describes by a nearby trail or lake, never colours the whole lake: it is a marker at the coordinates the Division gives, and without them it is listed but not drawn; an advisory listed with "N/A" for the day lifted is kept in the history without a period, since none is published, and a starred row is shown with this site's caution from the day posted, never as the Division's advisory. A closed swim beach is shown as the Department of Health's closure from the first day this site read it until the list no longer carries it; Jefferson Ridge West is listed but not drawn on the map: the Department's list gives no water or point, and its older inventory of swimming beaches names Dierks Lake in Sevier County, whose federal hydrography gives the reservoir no name to cite a point from. Mom's Lake's advisory is likewise listed but not drawn on the map: the Division gives no point and no federal place name of that lake was found. Location texts lose their house numbers and the filers' comments are not read; a report on private property or a basement backup keeps no location at all. The full reports are in the Division's search.
