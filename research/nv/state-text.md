**What the law requires.** Since 2025, a Nevada city, county, unincorporated town, general improvement district, wastewater district or water authority that knows of a sewage discharge in its area must notify the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection on the Division's website or by telephone; the law sets no deadline, and the Division must notify any Indian tribe that may be affected (NRS 445A.467). The Division issues Nevada's discharge permits, and the federal standard permit condition requires a report to it within 24 hours of a sewer overflow or a treatment plant bypass and in writing within five days (40 CFR 122.41(l)(6)); the permits themselves were not read. No Nevada statute or rule found requires the public to be told when a discharge happens, and the law sets no period after a discharge during which the water is to be avoided. Nevada has no combined sewers. Harmful algal bloom advisories are issued by the state's bloom task force; no law requires them.

**What is published.** No published sewage discharge report was found for Nevada; the operators' own websites were not searched one by one. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's overflow and bypass records hold no Nevada report and list no Nevada sewer system (its extracts of November 12, 2026). The Division of State Parks' HAB Advisory page lists the waters in state parks under a Watch, Warning or Danger advisory, each with the date of its last update: on November 15, 2026 it listed Watch advisories at Rye Patch Reservoir and Wild Horse Reservoir and a Warning at Lahontan Reservoir, and no Danger advisory. The Office of State Epidemiology lists every active bloom advisory in the state with its level and the date it took effect: on November 16, 2026 it listed eleven under eight headings, including advisories at Lake Mohave, Walker Lake, Virginia Lake, Mountain View Pond and South Meadows Community Pond outside the state parks. This site reads both for algal blooms. This site lists Nevada's 16 sewage discharge permits in the federal permit records, with their 35 discharge points: 13 operators hold 15 of them, and for one, the Truckee Meadows Water Reclamation Facility's, the records name only the plant, so it is listed by its permit number and not counted as an operator; the City of Sparks, whose sewers hold no permit of their own, is listed too, as is the Hoover Dam's plant on the Nevada side, under an Arizona permit.

**What is not published.** No list of the notices the Division receives under NRS 445A.467 or of the permit reports was found on its website when it was read on November 9, 2026, and its online reporting form shows the public no submitted report; the notices can be sought through a records request. The state's bloom dashboard offered no data download when it was read on November 9, 2026, and its weekly update goes by e-mail. The parks' page covers waters in state parks only and gives no date on which an advisory began or was lifted. The Office's list gives no lift date and no location beyond the water's name and, for one pond, its town. Lake Tahoe's nearshore bacteria monitoring publishes reports, not current results, and no current beach bacteria results were found for Nevada. No operator's overflow page was found, though the operators' websites were not searched one by one; Reno takes spill reports from the public by telephone.

**What stands in the way.** The 2025 law sends every notice to the state, and no duty to tell the public was found, so an overflow can leave no public trace unless an operator or the press announces it; the law followed a 2022 spill into the Truckee River from the City of Sparks's sewers that was not reported to the state at the time. A same-day public posting of the notices would need a change in law or a decision by the Division. A data export of the bloom dashboard would make bloom advisories across the state easier to follow.

**Known problems in the data.** The parks' page gives the date of an advisory's last update, not of its issue, and no time; it drops a water when the advisory is lifted, without a date. A water can move from one level to another, and the page's sections then change without notice. The parks' page and the state's list can give different dates for one advisory: Wild Horse Reservoir's was updated October 5, 2026 on the parks' page and in effect since August 13, 2026 on the list. The federal permit records give one of the 16 permits (Reno's Stead plant) as expired, name a company as the permittee of one federal installation (the Hawthorne Army Depot), and name only the plant for the Truckee Meadows Water Reclamation Facility. Nevada keeps Pacific time, except the City of West Wendover, which keeps Mountain time under the federal time-zone boundary (49 CFR 71.9).

**What this site does about it.** A bloom advisory is shown apart from sewage, as the parks' own Watch, Warning or Danger advisory, from the first reading of the page that listed it, since the page gives no issue date, until the first reading that no longer lists it; if the page cannot be read, the advisory ends here 14 days after it was last seen. A change of level is the same advisory at its new level. The parks' update date is shown as the date of the update. Each advisory on the Office of State Epidemiology's list is shown as the Office's own advisory at its level, from the date the Office gives, until the first reading of the list that no longer shows it. An advisory for a whole water colours that water; one for a cove is listed on its water without colouring all of it. An advisory on both the parks' page and the list is shown once, from the date the Office gives, at the stricter of the two levels, citing both. Virginia Lake, Mountain View Pond in Gardnerville and South Meadows Community Pond have no water record on this site, so their advisories are listed with the place the Office gives and are not drawn on the map. Times are shown in Pacific time, and in Mountain time at West Wendover. Nevada's sewage discharge permits and their discharge points are listed with their receiving waters; with no published reports, a water with no report on this site is shown grey, which means no report, not a finding about the water.
