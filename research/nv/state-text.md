**What the law requires.** Since 2025, a Nevada city, county, unincorporated town, general improvement district, wastewater district or water authority that knows of a sewage discharge in its area must notify the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection on the Division's website or by telephone; the law sets no deadline, and the Division must notify any Indian tribe that may be affected (NRS 445A.467). The Division issues Nevada's discharge permits, and the federal standard permit condition requires a report to it within 24 hours of a sewer overflow or a treatment plant bypass and in writing within five days (40 CFR 122.41(l)(6)); the permits themselves were not read. No Nevada statute or rule requires the public to be told when a discharge happens, and the law sets no period after a discharge during which the water is to be avoided. Nevada has no combined sewers. Harmful algal bloom advisories are issued by the state's bloom task force and, for waters in state parks, posted by the Division of State Parks; no law requires them.

**What is published.** No published sewage discharge report was found for Nevada; the operators' own websites were not searched one by one. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's overflow and bypass records hold no Nevada report and list no Nevada sewer system (its extracts of November 12, 2026). The Division of State Parks' HAB Advisory page lists the waters in state parks under a Watch, Warning or Danger advisory, each with the date of its last update and the parks' explanation: on August 13, 2026 (the Internet Archive's copy) it listed Watch advisories at Lahontan Reservoir, Rye Patch Reservoir, Marlette Lake and Washoe Lake, and in November 2025 a Warning at Lahontan Reservoir. This site reads that page for algal blooms. This site also lists Nevada's 16 sewage discharge permits in the federal permit records, with their 35 discharge points: 13 operators hold 15 of them, and for one, the Truckee Meadows Water Reclamation Facility's, the records name only the plant, so it is listed by its permit number and not counted as an operator; the City of Sparks, whose sewers hold no permit of their own, is listed too.

**What is not published.** No list of the notices the Division receives under NRS 445A.467 or of the permit reports was found on its website when it was read on November 9, 2026, and its online reporting form shows the public no submitted report; the notices can be sought through a records request. The state's bloom dashboard, the only statewide view of bloom advisories found, offered no data download when it was read on November 9, 2026, and its weekly update goes by e-mail only. The parks' page covers waters in state parks only and gives no date on which an advisory began or was lifted. Lake Tahoe's nearshore bacteria monitoring publishes reports, not current results, and no current beach bacteria results were found for Nevada. No operator's overflow page was found, though the operators' websites were not searched one by one; Reno takes spill reports from the public by telephone.

**What stands in the way.** The 2025 law sends every notice to the state and none to the public, so an overflow leaves no public trace unless the press reports it; the law followed a 2022 spill into the Truckee River from the City of Sparks's sewers that was not reported to the state at the time. A same-day public posting of the notices would need a change in law or a decision by the Division. A public export of the bloom dashboard would show bloom advisories outside the state parks. The parks' and the Division's websites did not answer this site's collector on November 14 and 15, 2026 (no connection could be opened); this site has not yet made a current reading of the parks' page, so the advisories named above are those of the archived copies, and none is shown as current until a reading succeeds.

**Known problems in the data.** The parks' page gives the date of an advisory's last update, not of its issue, and no time; it drops a water when the advisory is lifted, without a date. A water can move from one level to another, and the page's sections then change without notice. The federal permit records give one of the 16 permits (Reno's Stead plant) as expired, name a company as the permittee of one federal installation (the Hawthorne Army Depot), and name only the plant for the Truckee Meadows Water Reclamation Facility. Nevada keeps Pacific time, except the City of West Wendover, which keeps Mountain time under the federal time-zone boundary (49 CFR 71.9).

**What this site does about it.** A bloom advisory is shown apart from sewage, as the parks' own Watch, Warning or Danger advisory, from the first reading of the page that listed it, since the page gives no issue date, until the first reading that no longer lists it; if the page cannot be read, the advisory ends here 14 days after it was last seen. A change of level is the same advisory at its new level. The parks' update date is shown as the date of the update. Times are shown in Pacific time, and in Mountain time at West Wendover. Nevada's sewage discharge permits and their discharge points are listed with their receiving waters; with no published reports, a water with no report on this site is shown grey, which means no report, not a finding about the water.
