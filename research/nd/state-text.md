**What the law requires.** North Dakota's rules require a spill or discharge that causes, or is likely to cause, water pollution to be reported to the Department of Environmental Quality immediately, by telephone or online (N.D. Admin. Code 33.1-16-02.1-11(4)). Sewage permits add an oral report within 24 hours and a written one within five days of a discharge that may endanger health or the environment, lagoon overflows and bypasses included (the domestic-treatment general permit NDG420000, Part IV(F), effective 2023 to 2027, and the individual permits). No North Dakota statute or rule requires the public to be told of a sewage discharge, and none sets a period after one during which the water is to be avoided. A draft of Fargo's 2023 permit proposed immediate public notice of the overflows its plan identifies; whether the final permit kept it was not confirmed. North Dakota has no combined sewers.

**What is published.** The Department keeps a public archive of the incident reports it received from 1975 to 2020: 4,452 incidents, each with the day it happened and the day it was reported, the county, a map point, the material and, where given, an amount (7 of the 36 sewage reports give none). A search of the archive for sewage, sewer and septic finds 36 sewage reports from 2003 to 2020, 19 of them in Grand Forks County (up to 500,000 gallons, in September 2019). The Department lists the harmful algal bloom advisories and warnings it posts, by lake and county with the day posted, from May to December: on November 14, 2026, 14 advisories (a bloom is present) and 6 warnings (a bloom over much of the water with toxins above the recreational threshold), posted from June 29 to October 8, 2026.

**What is not published.** Since 2021 reports go into the state's unified spill reporting system; its public list of incidents refuses automated readers, so whether sewer overflows are entered in it, and how quickly, could not be checked. The archive gives no end, no receiving water and no responsible party, and stops at 2020. Permit factsheets list some cities' past overflows (Fargo, January 2019 to July 2023; Grand Forks, to September 2023), but Fargo's is a scanned image and neither is a current record. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's overflow records hold no North Dakota report. No statewide program samples swimming beaches for bacteria and posts the results.

**What stands in the way.** The state's incident list answers this site's reader with a block page (a bot wall), in November 2026 as before; it is not got around here. The Department's permit records system shows records only through a browser application. Reports to the Department reach no public channel by law, and the fix is an export of the incident list or a public-notice duty.

**Known problems in the data.** The archive's search covers every column but the dates, so sewage reports are found by the words of the material; two rows described only as "wastewater" at a site with a sugar factory, and industrial and food-processing wastewater, are left out. One incident appears twice. Its "contained" flag is not an end. The bloom listing gives the day each advisory was posted, not when the bloom began or was sampled, and no day it was lifted; it is a document whose text is split in pieces. Routine bloom monitoring for 2026 ended on September 30.

**What this site does about it.** An archived sewage report is placed by its own map point (kept to about 100 metres), dated to the day it happened, and a septic or on-site housing report is listed with its county only; these reports are history and colour no water today, and the incident filed twice is counted once. A bloom advisory or warning is shown as the Department's own advisory from the day it was posted for as long as the Department lists it, and ends at the first reading that no longer lists it; it is placed on the lake by its name within the county the Department gives, and marked at a point inside the lake's outline in the national hydrography data or, where that data draws none, at the lake's point in the national gazetteer of place names, only when one lake of that exact name lies in that county (15 of the 20 lakes listed on November 14, 2026). The place of the other five is not known: no lake of the names Buffalo Lake (Sargent County), Larimore Dam, South Golden Lake, Sykeston Dam or West Lake Napoleon is in either national dataset in the county the Department gives, and a point is not guessed; their advisories stay listed among this state's reports with their county, without a marker. Dates are read in Central time, and in Mountain time in Adams, Billings, Bowman, Dunn, Golden Valley, Grant, Hettinger, Slope and Stark counties (49 CFR 71.7(a)); McKenzie and Sioux counties are split by the line, and since the line inside them is not traced here, this site reads their reports in Mountain time, the later end of the day.
