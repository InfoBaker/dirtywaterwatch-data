**What the law requires.** The Oklahoma Department of Environmental Quality issues Oklahoma's discharge permits. The standard conditions of its municipal permits require the permittee to tell the Department orally within 24 hours of becoming aware of any sewer overflow or bypass, and in writing within five days unless the Department waives the written report. The Department's own instructions differ: its wastewater reporting page asks for a telephone report within 48 hours of the event and a written follow-up in five days (also written as five business days), and its bypass reporting form, revised in October 2025, asks for 24 hours and five days. No Oklahoma statute or rule requires the public to be told when a discharge happens, and the law sets no period after a discharge during which the water is to be avoided. Oklahoma has no combined sewers. On tribal land the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, not the state, may issue the permit; for the four tribal permits listed here the issuing agency was not read from the permits.

**What is published.** No sewage discharge report is published for Oklahoma. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's overflow and bypass records hold no Oklahoma report; they list one Oklahoma sewer system, the Kickapoo plant's, with no report (its extracts of November 12, 2026). This site lists the 288 operators of Oklahoma's 316 sewage discharge permits in the federal permit records, with their 404 discharge points, and a few private permits more; the Department's map of discharge points, updated June 14, 2026, gives the same points with each permit's status. The National Park Service posts weekly E. coli results for ten swimming places in Chickasaw National Recreation Area in summer, with its threshold (235 for a weekly result, 126 for the 30-day geometric mean): on November 14, 2026 the table held 40 results from August 6 to August 27, 2026, the highest 162.4 at Bear Falls on August 20, none at the threshold. The table states no unit.

**What is not published.** The Department posts no list of the overflow and bypass reports it receives; they reach the public only through an Open Records Act request. No operator was found to post its overflows on the web; Tulsa takes overflow reports from the public by telephone. The Park Service posts a sign at a swimming place when its results are high, but its page gives no date on which a sign was posted or removed. The state's lake conditions page, Tulsa's Zink Lake dashboard and the Grand River Dam Authority's water-quality map did not show their results as data; the Oklahoma Water Resources Board's monitoring data sits behind a sign-in; the Army Corps of Engineers' Tulsa District page refuses automated readers. No statewide beach monitoring program was found.

**What stands in the way.** Every report goes to the Department and none to the public, so an overflow leaves no public trace unless an operator or the press announces it. A public list of the reports, by the Department or the operators, would need a change in law or an agency decision, and the three differing reporting instructions would need to be reconciled. Lake and beach results would need to be published as data with their dates to be read.

**Known problems in the data.** The federal permit records sometimes name a plant instead of its owner, and for four permits (Avant, Forgan, Minco and Wetumka's two plants) the owner was not read; 17 of the 316 permits are marked terminated on the Department's map and nine are not on it. The Park Service's table gives sample dates without a unit, writes results below detection as "<1", and keeps one season's table on its page after the season ends. Oklahoma keeps Central time throughout.

**What this site does about it.** Oklahoma's sewage discharge permits and their discharge points are listed with their receiving waters; with no published reports, a water with no report on this site is shown grey, which means no report, not a finding about the water. A Park Service result at or above the park's threshold is shown as a beach result, apart from sewage, with this site's caution for 72 hours from the sample date, and never as the park's own notice, whose dates are not published; a result below the threshold is counted but not shown. Times are shown in Central time.
