**What the law requires.** Sanitary sewer overflows are prohibited in Tennessee, and every discharge permit requires the permittee to report any noncompliance or release that could threaten human health or the environment to the Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and in writing within five days (Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0400-40-05-.07(2)(n) and (o)). No Tennessee statute or rule requires a permittee or the Department to tell the public of an overflow, and the law sets no period after an overflow during which the water is to be avoided. Nashville, Chattanooga and Clarksville have combined sewers whose overflows are discharges their permits allow. The Department posts streams with high bacteria levels under a water-contact advisory and issues advisories for harmful algal blooms.

**What is published.** The Department publishes the reports filed through its online forms in its Division of Water Resources Events Report, a public table with a one-click download: 12,857 reports of sewer overflows, releases, bypasses, treatment upsets and washouts from the onboarding of municipal systems in 2020 and 2021 to October 29, 2026 (read November 3, 2026), 2,802 of them in 2025. Each report gives the permittee and permit, the county, the city and street of the event, the type (wet- or dry-weather overflow or release, bypass, upset, extreme event, unpermitted discharge, washout), the estimated volume, the day it began and, on about one report in seven, the day it ended. Reports reach the table about five days after the event began. Nashville's Metro Water Services filed 1,242 of them, Clarksville 908 and Chattanooga 439. The Department's harmful algal bloom layer lists 71 bloom reports with the Department's finding and 21 advisories (18 precautionary, 3 no-contact) issued from March 2023 to September 2026, each with its start and end dates; none was in effect on November 7, 2026. The Department's list of posted streams (revised August 2026) names 25 stream segments under a standing water-contact advisory for bacteria, 21 of them with sewer collection systems or a sewage plant among the sources, including the Cumberland River in Nashville from Bordeaux Bridge (river mile 185.7) to the Woodland Street Bridge (river mile 190.6) and Knoxville's First, Second, Third and Goose creeks. Each report in the table also says whether it was an overflow or a "release": by the Department's definition a release is sewage from the collection system "that does not reach waters", including a backup into a building (Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0400-40-05-.02(75)).

**What is not published.** The Events Report gives no time of day, no receiving water and no coordinates; the Department's events map shows points, but its data could not be read. It has no reports from the City of Memphis's two treatment plants. It does not include combined sewer overflows, and no public notice of a combined sewer overflow event was found for Nashville, Chattanooga or Clarksville; their permits' own terms for telling the public were not read. The filed report of each event, which may name the receiving water, is kept in the Department's document viewer one file at a time. The posted-streams list gives no date on which each posting began. Chattanooga and the Hamilton County Water and Wastewater Treatment Authority publish quarterly consent decree reports with each overflow as PDF files. The operators' own pages were not searched one by one.

**What stands in the way.** No law requires anyone to tell the public of an overflow, so the Department's table, about five days late and without the water affected, is the only public record of most overflows. On November 7, 2026 the Department's data server refused this site's automated requests, so the reports shown here wait until it accepts them. The table carries the Department's disclaimer that it "is provided for informational purposes only and should not be used to determine compliance". This site reads only the advisory fields of the Department's algal bloom layer, never the details of the people who report blooms.

**Known problems in the data.** The Events Report has no report number, so a report is identified by its permit, start day, type and street. Most reports, 11,104 of 12,857, give no end date. Memphis's plants are absent, although the city's sewers overflow and have been under a federal consent decree since 2012; how its reports reach the Department is not known. Six bloom reports the Department has not yet assessed give no status date, only the day the bloom was seen.

**What this site does about it.** A report is shown from its start date to its end date, then with this site's caution (72 hours; 96 on lakes, ponds and reservoirs), since the law sets no period; where a duration is given, the end is the last day it allows, or the end date when that is later; a report with no end date is shown with this site's caution for 72 hours from the start of the day the event began, then with its end not confirmed until 30 days after it began. Upsets, washouts, extreme events and unpermitted discharges are shown as sewage reports like the rest; a release, which by the Department's definition reached no water, is never placed on a water. Because the table names no water and gives no point, every report is listed with its county, city and permittee and is not placed on any water: this site does not place it at the operator's plant and does not guess a location from the street. Dates are read in Eastern time in the thirty counties of East Tennessee and in Central time elsewhere. Each posted stream is shown as the Department's own water-contact advisory, in its words, for as long as the list carries it, and only on the stream it names, in its county: a marker at the stream's mouth (for the Cumberland River in Nashville, at the Woodland Street Bridge, the posting's downstream end), never a colour on the river or lake the stream flows into. Where the stream has no record on this site yet, the marker alone shows the posting. The list gives no start date, so a posting is shown from the first day this site read it. The Department's algal bloom advisories are shown as its own, from the start date to the end date it gives; a bloom reported with no advisory, whether the Department confirmed it or has not yet assessed it, is shown with this site's caution from the day it was seen, never as the Department's advisory. Where the Department marks a bloom's water a private residence or farm pond, only its county is shown. The combined sewer outfalls of Nashville, Chattanooga and Clarksville carry this site's rain caution after heavy rain. House numbers and personal names in the reports are not reproduced here; the full reports are in the Department's table.
