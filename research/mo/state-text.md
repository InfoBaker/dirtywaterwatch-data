**What the law requires.** Missouri's permitted wastewater facilities must report a sanitary sewer overflow or bypass from their treatment works or collection system to the Department of Natural Resources within 24 hours of discovering it, through the Department's online reporting portal (the Department's sewer overflow guidance and the permits' standard conditions). No Missouri statute or rule requiring the public to be told of an overflow was found, and none sets a period after one during which the water is to be avoided. Combined sewer operators (sewage and rainwater in one pipe) are expected to notify the public under their permits and consent decrees (the Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District's of 2011 and Kansas City's of 2010), whose notification clauses were not read for this page; both post signs at their outfalls. At state park swim beaches the Department posts "Swimming is Not Recommended" when the weekly E. coli samples exceed the state standard (RSMo 640.080).

**What is published.** Kansas City's KC Water posts some of its overflows as news: 99 posts from July 2016 to August 2025, covering 93 overflows, most with the time crews responded or the overflow began, the time it ended, the gallons and the creek or river reached. St. Louis's Metropolitan Sewer District (MSD Project Clear) posts some of its overflows and sewer breaks as alerts and news releases: 89 posts from June 2013 to January 2026, covering 79 overflows, with the day the overflow was found, often the creek reached and sometimes the gallons. The Department's monthly Water Pollution Control concern reports list the concerns it investigated: six reports from December 2025 to May 2026 held 945 entries (a concern investigated over more than one month appears in each month's report), 140 of them about a bypass or sewer overflow, on 131 distinct concerns. The Department's harmful algal bloom map shows the blooms reported to it on public waters: on November 15, 2026 it held 283 reports from 2017 on, 39 of them shown as a harmful algal bloom present. The Department's outfall map layer gives the location and receiving stream of 146 combined sewer outfalls on five permits, including 120 of MSD's Lemay plant that the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's inventory lists as closed or not at all.

**What is not published.** The overflow reports operators send the Department are not published; the concern reports show an overflow only when someone raised a concern about it, and only months later. KC Water and MSD post some overflows, not every one, and give no end of any advice; MSD gives no time of day for most and no end time. KC Water has posted no overflow since August 2025. The concern reports give a county and dates, but no point, end or volume. The state park beach results, sampled weekly from Memorial Day to Labor Day, are published as a data set on the state's open data portal; we check that it is available but do not yet show its results. On November 15, 2026 all 17 beaches were closed for the season, so no beach advisory stood. The bloom map gives the day a bloom was observed but no date for its advisory or its end, and leaves out privately owned waters.

**What stands in the way.** The 24-hour reports are filed in a portal behind a login and can be seen only through a records request. The concern reports come out with the meetings of the state's water commission, two to four months after the month they cover. The Department's environmental emergency searches have no export. KC Water's Smart Sewer site refuses automated reading.

**Known problems in the data.** KC Water's posts often give the time crews responded rather than when the overflow began. MSD's posts give weekdays ("discovered Tuesday morning") rather than dates. A concern is the concerned party's account: the Department's comments say what it found for some concerns and not for others. Names in the posts sometimes repeat with different spellings ("Kiefer Creek" and "Keifer Creek").

**What we do about it.** A KC Water post is dated to the time it gives; most give the time crews responded, which is the latest the overflow can have begun, not necessarily its start, and an end is used only when the post states one. An MSD post is dated to the day the overflow was found (the weekday it names, on or before the post), and a post naming no day is dated to its own day, the latest the overflow can have begun. Because neither operator publishes when its advice ends, a report is shown with our caution. A concern is kept when the operator reported the overflow itself or the Department's comments say what it found; one the Department found unsupported, or that concerned a private septic system, is left out; a concern from the public with no finding stated is shown as the public's report, with our caution only. A bloom the Department's map shows as present, or a water it shows as closed, is shown as the Department's advisory from the first time we read it so until the first read that no longer does, both our reads, not the Department's dates, at the map's own point; the day the bloom was observed is kept beside it. The overflow reports carry no map point: a report naming a water is placed on that water within the operator's counties or the concern's county, and one naming none is listed with its operator or county and placed on no water. The concern texts, which name people and private homes, are not reproduced, and house numbers in the operators' posts are left out; each report links its source. Combined sewer outfalls are shown from the Department's layer where it has them, else from EPA's inventory, with the rain caution at each.
