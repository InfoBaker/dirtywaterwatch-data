**What the law requires.** The Department of Environmental Quality asks whoever owns or runs a sewer system or treatment works to report a sanitary sewer overflow to it verbally within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and in writing within five days with the corrective actions taken. No Wyoming statute, rule or permit found requires the public to be told of an overflow, and none sets a period after one during which the water is to be avoided. Permits on the Wind River Reservation are issued by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and their reports go to it.

**What is published.** The Department of Environmental Quality publishes a list of the spill reports it has closed, of every substance, as a download from its [spill reporting portal](https://deqspills.wyo.gov/). Each report gives the incident's date and time, the county and town, the substance, the quantity, whether the spill reached water, the water and a map point. On November 16, 2026, the list held 6,066 reports. By our reading, 84 were sewage spills, from February 2010 to September 23, 2026, including 18 in 2025 and 12 in 2026; 31 of the 84 were marked as reaching water. The list also holds 81 reports of harmful cyanobacteria blooms. The Department of Health and the Department of Environmental Quality issue recreational use advisories for harmful cyanobacteria blooms: a bloom advisory for a water and a toxin advisory for a location on it. Each new advisory, and a lift, goes out as a public bulletin carrying the table of current advisories with the date each was issued. On November 15, 2026 the bulletin feed held 25 bulletins from July 31 to October 28, 2026, with 71 advisories at 41 waters; the latest table, of October 28, listed 66 (42 bloom advisories and 24 toxin advisories at 40 waters). An advisory holds until the bloom has dissipated or the toxins are below the recreational levels, or until December 31, whichever comes first.

**What is not published.** The overflow reports operators send the Department under its sewer overflow rule are not published. An overflow appears on the spill list only when it was also reported to the Department's spill line, so the list does not hold every overflow. The list shows a report once the Department closes it, a median of 18 days after the incident for the sewage reports, and gives neither the time the report was made nor an end. The Environmental Protection Agency's overflow tables (of November 13, 2026, read on November 15, 2026) hold no Wyoming report. The Department's 2024 notices about the Hudson wastewater release return a server error. The agencies' list of blooms under investigation is not in the bulletins and is not shown. No statewide beach bacteria program was found.

**What stands in the way.** There is no public list of the overflow reports operators send under the sewer overflow rule and no duty to tell the public. The Department's spill system can be searched only with a login, so a records request is the only way to see the overflow reports that are not on the spill list.

**Known problems in the data.** On the spill list, the columns for whether a spill could reach water and whether it reached water give the same answer on every report. Of the 84 sewage reports, 18 give a date with no time, 20 of the 31 marked as reaching water name no water, and seven give a point outside Wyoming or outside the county the report names. The filer types the substance, so sewage appears under many names, and some reports of wastewater do not say whether it was sewage. One report number is used for two spills in different counties. The bloom bulletins come when something changes, so weeks can pass between them, and the feed keeps only its latest 25. Within the feed, one advisory was lifted by a bulletin (Lake DeSmet, August 21); the agencies renamed the location of three advisories and reissued one with a new date. The agencies said in October that sampling ends with October and that new advisories will be sent as bulletins, with changes shown on their map, so a lift may come without a bulletin.

**What we do about it.** We read the spill list for sewage by rule: a report whose substance names sewage, septage, a sanitary sewer or the like, or whose description says sewage was released. Spills of oil, fuel, produced water, chemicals, industrial or treated wastewater, odour complaints and wastewater a report does not tie to a sewer are not shown. We date a sewage report by the incident date and time it gives, in Mountain time. The list gives no end, so we show our caution from that time and then the end as not confirmed until 30 days after it. A report marked as reaching water is placed on the water it names, within its county, or at its own point; a report marked unknown is placed only on a water it names; one marked as not reaching water is listed with its county and colours no water. A tributary or drainage of a named water is a different water, and the report is not placed on the water it names. We show a point only for public sewer works, rounded to about 100 meters, and only when it lies in the county the report gives. We name the operator only when the list names a public system on our operator list; the list's responsible parties, addresses and descriptions can name private people and are not shown. A report that leaves the list stays here as a past report. We show a bloom advisory from the date the agencies issued it. The latest bulletin's table is the agencies' current list: an advisory on it is shown as in effect until a later bulletin drops it or lifts it, or December 31 passes, the end of season the program states. An advisory dropped from a later table ends at that bulletin, and one the agencies lifted ends at the lift bulletin. A bulletin whose table shows no advisories is not read as ending them. A bloom advisory colours its lake or reservoir; a toxin advisory names a location, and an advisory for one part of a water is not shown over the whole water: where the bulletin gives no point for that part, it is listed with its water. An advisory on a river is listed with the river, which is not coloured. A water we have no record of is listed by name, with no water coloured. The bulletins name staff, so we keep only their tables. Dates are read in Mountain time. We show the treatment-plant outfalls of Wyoming's 90 permit holders.
