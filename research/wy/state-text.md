**What the law requires.** The Department of Environmental Quality asks whoever owns or runs a sewer system or treatment works to report a sanitary sewer overflow to it by telephone within 24 hours of becoming aware of it, and in writing within five days with the corrective actions taken. No Wyoming statute, rule or permit found requires the public to be told of an overflow, and none sets a period after one during which the water is to be avoided. Permits on the Wind River Reservation are issued by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and their reports go to it.

**What is published.** The Department of Health and the Department of Environmental Quality issue recreational use advisories for harmful cyanobacteria blooms, a bloom advisory for a water and a toxin advisory for a location on it, and send each new advisory and each lift as a public bulletin carrying the table of current advisories with the date each was issued. On November 15, 2026 the bulletin feed held 25 bulletins from July 31 to October 28, 2026: 71 advisories at 41 waters, of which the latest table, of October 28, listed 66 (42 bloom advisories and 24 toxin advisories at 40 waters). An advisory holds until the bloom has dissipated or the toxins are below the recreational levels, or until December 31, whichever comes first.

**What is not published.** The overflow reports operators send the Department are not published anywhere, and the Environmental Protection Agency's overflow tables held no Wyoming report on November 14, 2026, so no Wyoming sewage overflow is shown here. The Department's 2024 notices about the Hudson wastewater release could not be read in this build. The Department's list of blooms under investigation is not in the bulletins and is not shown. No statewide beach bacteria program was found.

**What stands in the way.** There is no public list of the overflow reports, and no duty to tell the public; the Department's spill system can be searched only with a login, so a records request to the Department is the only way to see them.

**Known problems in the data.** Most advisories leave the bulletins' table without a lift bulletin of their own: of the 5 advisories that ended within the feed, one was lifted by a bulletin and 4 simply stopped being listed. The bulletins come when something changes, so weeks can pass between them; the feed keeps only its latest 25 bulletins.

**What this site does about it.** An advisory is shown from the date the agencies issued it. The latest bulletin's table is the agencies' current list: an advisory on it is shown as in effect until a later bulletin drops it or lifts it, or December 31 passes, the end of season the program states. An advisory dropped from a later table ends at that bulletin, and one the agencies lifted ends at the lift bulletin. A bulletin whose table shows no advisories is not read as ending them. An advisory for one part of a water is not shown over the whole water; where the bulletin gives no point for that part, its place on the map is not known and it is listed with its water. The bulletins name staff, so only their tables are kept. An advisory is placed by the water's name in Wyoming, or by the point the agencies give for an unnamed lake. Dates are read in Mountain time. The treatment-plant outfalls of Wyoming's 90 permit holders are shown, and their reports are listed when they become public.
