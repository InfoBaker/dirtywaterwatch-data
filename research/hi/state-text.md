**What the law requires.** Whoever owns or runs a wastewater system in Hawaii must report a spill, overflow or discharge to the Department of Health (Hawaii Administrative Rules, chapter 11-62, Appendix B); wastewater discharge permits issued under chapter 11-55 add their own reporting conditions. No Hawaii rule requiring the operator itself to tell the public was found. The Department does: it posts an advisory on its website, puts up warning signs at the affected waters, sends e-mail notices by island and, for large spills, issues news releases, and it advises the public to remain out of the affected waters until it cancels the advisory. No fixed period after a discharge is set during which the water is to be avoided. Hawaii has no combined sewers (sewage and rainwater in one pipe).

**What is published.** The Department lists every advisory it issues on its website, open and closed, in a form a computer can read. On November 7, 2026 the list returned the newest 500 of the 1,901 advisories it holds, posted from January 1, 2023: 50 sewage spill advisories, 15 permit exceedance advisories (a treatment plant's discharge above the bacteria limit of its permit), 279 Brown Water Advisories (storm-water runoff after heavy rain), 52 beach advisories (enterococci bacteria above the Department's threshold at a monitored beach) and 104 beach notifications (a high first sample that the Department is retesting). Each advisory gives the island, the place, the time it was posted and the time it was cancelled; sewage notices usually name the affected waters and often the volume and the cause. Of the 65 sewage spill and permit exceedance advisories, 57 were on Oahu, four on Hawaii Island and two each on Maui and Kauai. A sewage spill advisory stayed in effect a median of 6.7 days.

**What is not published.** The operators' reports to the Department are not published; only the Department's advisories are. The list gives the advisory's posting and cancellation as times, not when a spill began or ended. It gives no coordinates: places are named in words. The list returns only its newest 500 advisories, so older ones are not read here. Whether the Department receives reports that lead to no advisory is not stated. The operators' own web pages were not searched one by one.

**What stands in the way.** With no duty on operators to tell the public, the Department's advisories are the public record of sewage spills in Hawaii. The list is published without stated terms of use. Warning signs are seen only at the water, and the e-mail notices reach only their subscribers.

**Known problems in the data.** The list gives times without a time zone; this site reads them as Coordinated Universal Time, which places the postings in the Department's working day in Honolulu, and shows them in Hawaii time. Places are written by hand: the same water appears with and without Hawaiian spelling marks (Kaneohe and Kāneʻohe), and two islands each have a Waimea Bay and a Kailua Bay. Twelve of the 65 sewage advisories name no water, only a street, a pump station or a treatment plant, and 18 give a street address. A volume is given as a number of gallons in 16 of the 65. Three beach notices give a sample date that cannot be right (one from 2001 for a notice posted in 2023).

**What this site does about it.** Sewage spill and permit exceedance advisories are shown as sewage reports in the Department's own window, from the time it posts the advisory until the time it cancels it; this site adds no period of its own after a cancellation. An advisory that leaves the Department's list without a cancellation is shown in effect up to the last time the list showed it, and then as "end not confirmed" until 30 days after it was posted. Brown Water Advisories are shown as their own advisories, apart from sewage reports and never in the sewage colours, for as long as the Department lists them. Beach advisories are shown as beach advisories; a beach notification gets this site's caution for 72 hours from the day of the sample, never as the Department's advisory. Each advisory is placed on the water it names (a bay, harbor, stream, canal or pond) when this site holds a record of that water on the same island; nothing is placed by guesswork from a street address. Advisories that name only a beach or a shore, the 32 that cover a whole island or the state, and the sewage reports that name no water are listed on this page with no water. Street addresses and house numbers are not reproduced here. The rain-based caution this site computes elsewhere is not computed for Hawaii: the radar rainfall it uses covers only the contiguous United States.
