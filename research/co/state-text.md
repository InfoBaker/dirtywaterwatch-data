**What the law requires.** Colorado law requires anyone whose activity results in a spill or discharge that may pollute state waters to notify the Water Quality Control Division of the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment as soon as they know of it (C.R.S. 25-8-601(2)), and the state's discharge permits require an oral report to the Division within 24 hours of any noncompliance that may endanger health or the environment and a written report within five days. The Department's reporting list names "All sanitary sewer releases". No Colorado statute or rule found requires the public to be told of a sewage spill, and none sets a period after one during which the water is to be avoided. Colorado has no combined sewers. Permits on tribal land and for federal facilities are issued by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, and their reports go to it.

**What is published.** The Department keeps a public sheet of algal bloom conditions for selected lakes and reservoirs: on November 14, 2026 it held 198 rows for 129 waters, a row added when a water's condition changes (Caution, Warning, Third party or No bloom reported), the last dated September 9, 2026; read as 21 bloom advisories, 17 of them still the water's last row. A large sewage spill reaches the public when an agency issues an advisory: Colorado Parks and Wildlife's release of August 30, 2026 reported about 4.2 million gallons of untreated sewage entering the Arkansas River in Pueblo on August 26 and 27 and advised visitors to avoid contact with the Arkansas River and John Martin Reservoir at the state park from August 31. The National Park Service announced toxic algae in Blue Mesa Reservoir's Iola Basin on August 13, 2026, and Barr Lake State Park's page has shown the reservoir on red alert for toxic algae since August 3, 2026.

**What is not published.** The spill and noncompliance reports operators send the Division are not published anywhere, and the Environmental Protection Agency's overflow tables held no Colorado report on November 14, 2026, so most spills, and every small one, are not seen here. The advisory after the Pueblo spill gave no end, and no lifting was found. The bloom sheet gives no time of day, no point and no sample results, and does not say whether its date is the sampling or the posting day. Closures of swim beaches for E. coli are listed only on the Department's page.

**What stands in the way.** There is no public list of the reports the law requires, and no duty to tell the public; a records request to the Department is the only way to see them. The Department's web pages refused this site's automated reader on November 14, 2026, and the City of Pueblo's site did not answer it, so the city's own account of its August spill could not be read.

**Known problems in the data.** The bloom sheet's latest rows date from early September, so a water it still lists under Caution or Warning may have cleared without a new row. One row is dated 2024 between August 2026 rows of the same reservoir and is kept as written. The sheet's "Third party" condition means another body reports for that water, and its level is not stated.

**What this site does about it.** A bloom advisory from the sheet is shown from the date of its first row until the sheet's next "No bloom reported" row for that water, as the Department's own condition; while it is still the water's last row it stays shown, and if the sheet stops showing it the advisory ends at that read. "No bloom reported" is shown as no report, never as clean water. Each official notice is read again at every refresh against the sentences it rests on; a notice whose page no longer carries them, or carries them changed, is kept as it was and checked again, never dropped: the Pueblo spill is shown on the Arkansas River with its dates and volume as the notice gives them, then with this site's caution (72 hours; 96 on lakes, ponds and reservoirs); an advisory with no end is shown for 14 days from its start unless the agency confirms it again; Blue Mesa Reservoir's caution, whose posting day the release does not give, is shown from the release day, the latest it can have begun; and Barr Lake's alert is shown while the park's page shows it, ends at the first read whose page no longer mentions it, and is shown as a new alert if the page shows it again. Dates are read in Mountain time. A water named in a notice is matched only to a water in the county the notice gives. The treatment-plant outfalls of Colorado's 324 permit holders are shown, and their reports are listed when they become public. Not yet read here: the Department's swim beach closures and news releases, and the City of Pueblo's notices, once their pages can be reached.
