**What the law requires.** Kentucky's regulation on discharge permits (401 KAR 5:065, Section 2(5)) requires a permittee to tell the Division of Water immediately, by telephone, of a spill, bypass, overflow or other event that may present an imminent or substantial danger to public health or the environment, and to follow with the federal written reports. No Kentucky statute or rule requires the public to be told when sewage is discharged, and the law sets no period after a discharge during which the water is to be avoided. Kentucky has 13 combined sewer systems (sewage and rainwater in one pipe) with 289 open outfalls in the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's national inventory (October 31, 2026; the Division of Water's own map lists 286 on November 7, 2026), most of them Louisville's and Northern Kentucky's. Federal policy expects combined sewer permits to require public notification of overflows; the Kentucky permits' own terms were not read for this page. Two operators publish notices: the Louisville and Jefferson County Metropolitan Sewer District (MSD) and Sanitation District No. 1 of Northern Kentucky (SD1).

**What is published.** MSD shows an Overflow Advisory Level on every page of its website. After rain it posts "MSD-01 Wet Weather Overflows", asking people to avoid contact with the Ohio River, streams, drainage ditches and standing water "until 48 hours after the rain has stopped"; it also posts MSD-02 notices of a single discharge, with the time it began, the pump station or sewer's address, the receiving stream and the amount. SD1 publishes its rule: from May through October it alerts its subscribers when 0.25 inch of rain falls in Boone, Campbell and Kenton counties or the Ohio River rises above 38 feet, and its advisories stand for 72 hours; signs at its outfalls give the same advice. Kentucky's own reporting system sent its overflow and bypass reports to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's overflow records for two months: the agency's extract of November 4, 2026 holds 43 Kentucky reports from 37 permits, begun between December 8, 2025 and January 8, 2026, each with a start and end to the minute and a map point, received a median of the same day. None has arrived since. The Division of Water's map of harmful algal blooms gives each sampled lake's status and toxin results (on November 7, 2026, a recreational advisory at Old Springfield Reservoir). The Division and the Department for Public Health keep standing swimming advisories, in place for years, on stretches of the Upper Cumberland River and its forks around Harlan and Pineville, the North Fork of the Kentucky River above Chavies, and the Licking River below Banklick Creek with all of Banklick and Three Mile creeks.

**What is not published.** The Division of Water publishes none of the reports it receives by telephone, nor the Environmental Response Team's incident records or the combined sewer operators' annual reports; they reach the public only through an open records request. MSD removes its notices after a while, and its banner carries no time. SD1's alerts go only to subscribers. Lexington's sewers are under a federal consent decree, but no public overflow notice was found; the other operators' own pages were not searched one by one. The swimming advisories page gives no dates and no map of the stretches. The Ohio River Valley Water Sanitation Commission's weekly bacteria results for the Ohio at Louisville and Cincinnati could not be read.

**What stands in the way.** The only legal channel is a telephone call to the state. Kentucky's reports reached the federal records for December 2025 and January 2026 and then stopped, so a month with no Kentucky report says nothing about whether sewage was discharged. The Ohio River commission's site refuses automated reading.

**Known problems in the data.** Every Kentucky report in the federal records is filed as a treatment plant bypass, although most describe a manhole, force main or pump station overflowing in the sewers. Most give the receiving water as "Unnamed Tributary or Unknown". The reports' descriptions name the people who called in and responded. One Louisville report gives an end 14 minutes before its start, and one incident is filed under two permits.

**What this site does about it.** A federal report is shown from its start to its end, then with this site's caution (72 hours; 96 on lakes, ponds and reservoirs), since the law sets no period. A report whose description names a manhole, force main, pump station or broken line is shown as a sewer overflow, the type as filed kept with it. A report is shown on a water when it names one, or when it answers that sewage reached an unnamed tributary or a creek; a report that names no water and does not say one was reached, or one that says no waterway was affected, is not shown on this site (20 of the 43 are shown); the federal record has every report. A sewer overflow whose map point is the treatment plant's own point (within 1 kilometre, 0.6 mile) is not placed at the plant: it is shown on the water it names, or listed with no water. MSD's banner is read every hour: MSD-01 is shown as MSD's own advisory, in its words, at each of MSD's open combined sewer outfalls, from the first reading that showed it until the first that no longer does, then with this site's caution, since MSD's advice runs until 48 hours after the rain stops; it is counted as one advisory, never as a discharge; an MSD-02 notice is shown as a dated report with MSD's own times, and with no end until MSD gives one. SD1's rule is applied from radar rainfall: for rain that begins in May through October (Kentucky time), 0.25 inch in 24 hours at SD1's outfalls brings a caution on the waters below them for 72 hours after the rain; elsewhere, and for rain that begins outside those months, this site's default rainfall caution applies; the river-stage part of SD1's rule is not applied. The standing swimming advisories are shown as the agencies' own, in their words, on each water the page names, for as long as the page lists them; the page gives no date, so they are shown from the first day this site read them, and the stretches are marked on their waters rather than drawn. Algal bloom advisories are shown apart from sewage, as the Division's own, for as long as its map shows them. Times are shown in Eastern time, and in Central time in the 41 western counties. Names, telephone numbers and house numbers in the reports' descriptions are not reproduced here.
