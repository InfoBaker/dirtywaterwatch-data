North Carolina has no combined sewers; its reported sewage discharges are sanitary sewer overflows and bypasses. State law (G.S. 143-215.1C) requires the operator of a wastewater system to send a press release to the news media covering the county within 24 hours of finding that 1,000 gallons or more of untreated wastewater reached surface waters, and to publish a newspaper notice for 15,000 gallons or more. Neither notice has to be posted online, and the law sets no period after a discharge during which the water should be avoided, so this site applies its own caution once a discharge ends. Operators must also report each overflow to the Division of Water Resources within 24 hours, and the Division publishes these reports on its public Sanitary Sewer Overflow Tracking Map. That map is the record shown here, from September 2022 onward. Privately owned systems (utility companies, property owners' associations, mobile-home parks and industrial sites) are bound by the same law and are listed like public ones, under the permittee the Division's permit records name; a few reports name no permit and are shown without an operator. Each report gives the start and end times, the estimated volume and the volume that reached water, the stream as the Division names it, and a location. Where the operator's written five-day report differs from the first report, the site uses the earlier start, the later end and the larger volume. Entries can reach the map a few days after the event. Some reports give no end time, and the 2022 to 2025 history gives none. The map does not show whether the press release was sent. On the coast, the Division of Marine Fisheries posts swimming advisories and storm-water precautionary advisories for its monitoring stations. Its map shows only the current status, with no issue or lift dates, so the site dates each advisory from when it saw the map change: these dates are the site's own observation, not dates published by the Division, and an advisory may have been issued earlier. When an advisory has not been reconfirmed for 14 days, the site stops colouring it. Algal bloom reports to the Division of Water Resources are shown as reports, not advisories, for seven days after the bloom was seen; their locations are rounded to about 100 metres. A water with no report is grey, which means only that nothing has been reported.
