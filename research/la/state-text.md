**What the law requires.** Louisiana's rules on unauthorized discharges (LAC 33:I Chapter 39) require an emergency discharge to be reported to the State Police hotline within the first hour, a discharge above a reportable quantity within 24 hours, and a written report to the Department of Environmental Quality within seven days. Nothing requires the public to be told, and the law sets no period after a discharge during which the water is to be avoided. Louisiana has no municipal combined sewer system. The City of Baton Rouge and Parish of East Baton Rouge, New Orleans' Sewerage and Water Board and the City of Shreveport work under federal consent decrees over their sewer overflows.

**What is published.** Baton Rouge's quarterly reports to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency and the Department list every discharge from the City-Parish's two collection systems in the quarter, with its day, cause, receiving water and gallons. This site reads the reports from October 2024 on: five of the eight pass this site's check that the rows read match the report's own count, giving 219 discharges from April 2025 to September 2026 (all but one dated in those quarters), 117 of them naming a receiving water. The Sewerage and Water Board publishes its consent-decree reports, which hold no dated list of overflows. Shreveport publishes yearly reports with a table of its overflows.

**What is not published.** The Department keeps the reports it receives in its document system, one document at a time, behind a CAPTCHA, and publishes no list; the federal overflow records hold no Louisiana report. Baton Rouge's reports give no time of day, no end and no map point, and list its treatment plants' discharges (one to eight a quarter) only as scanned forms. No other operator publishes its overflows that this site could read.

**What stands in the way.** A report is published once a quarter, about a month after the quarter ends, so a discharge appears here one to four months after it happened (73 days at the median). The Department of Health's beach monitoring and algal bloom pages refuse automated reading, so Louisiana's beach advisories are not shown here. Shreveport's report site did not answer this site on November 14, 2026.

**Known problems in the data.** Three of Baton Rouge's eight quarterly reports since October 2024 (October to December 2024, January to March 2025 and April to June 2026) are not shown, because the rows this site reads from them do not match the reports' own counts. Nearly half the discharges (102 of 219) name no receiving water. One row is dated a year before the quarter its report covers.

**What this site does about it.** A Baton Rouge discharge is shown with this site's caution for 72 hours from the start of the day it gives, then as "end not confirmed" until 30 days after it began, since the report gives no end and the law sets no period. A discharge that names a water is placed on that water in East Baton Rouge Parish; one that names none is listed with the City-Parish's permit and placed on no water. Grey means no report, never clean water.
